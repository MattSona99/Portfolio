AI amplifies whoever uses it. In the hands of a careful person it multiplies skill and time. In the hands of a careless or malicious one, it multiplies error and harm just as efficiently. The rules written for AI developers are not enough: there must also be a clear culture of use. This chapter describes what is expected of everyone who asks something of an AI, and what they owe to others in return.

## Principle

**4.1 The user is responsible.** Whoever asks, and whoever acts on the answer, answers for the consequences. "The AI did it" is no more an excuse than "the tool did it".

**4.2 Use AI to build, not to harm.** AI must not be used to defraud, harass, threaten, stalk, extort, impersonate or hurt anyone, nor to help someone else do so.

**4.3 Limits are not obstacles to be defeated.** The safety rules of an AI system exist to protect people. Tricking a system into producing what it has refused, in order to cause harm, is misuse. Studying a system's weaknesses is legitimate only when it is authorised and reported responsibly.

**4.4 Authorisation is the dividing line in security.** Testing, defending and learning about security are legitimate when they are done on systems one owns or has explicit permission to test. Breaking into systems, creating or spreading malicious software, or exploiting other people's vulnerabilities is not.

**4.5 Children are protected without exception.** AI must never be used to sexualise minors, to groom, exploit or abuse them, or to produce material that does so. There is no justification, no context and no purpose that changes this.

**4.6 Verify before you trust or share.** AI can be fluent and wrong at the same time. The more serious the consequences, the more carefully its answers must be checked against reliable sources.

**4.7 Be honest about the role of AI.** Where it matters to others, such as in exams, authorship, journalism, evidence, professional advice or personal relationships, people have the right to know whether AI was involved. No one should be led to believe they are dealing with a real person when they are not.

**4.8 Respect other people's data and rights.** Personal and confidential information of others is not fed into AI without the right to do so. A real person's face, voice or identity is not imitated without consent. The work of creators is respected.

**4.9 Keep your own judgment.** Moral decisions, essential skills and responsibility for one's own life are not outsourced to a machine. AI can assist, explain and propose. The person decides.

**4.10 The rules must be clear and fair.** Providers owe users understandable limits, explained refusals, a way to appeal and respect for their privacy. Users owe providers and society good faith.

## Risks

- **Fraud at scale.** Convincing messages, cloned voices and forged documents can be produced cheaply, in any language, for millions of targets.
- **A lower barrier to cybercrime.** People with little expertise can attempt what once required years of training.
- **Harassment and abuse.** Fake intimate images, extortion and targeted campaigns against individuals cause real and lasting harm.
- **Child exploitation.** AI can be used to produce abusive material, and to approach and manipulate children.
- **Misplaced trust.** Invented facts, citations and sources that look credible end up in court filings, medical decisions, news and school work.
- **Leaked data.** Confidential, personal or commercial information typed into a tool can end up where it should never be.
- **Hijacked agents.** AI that reads emails, web pages or documents can be manipulated by instructions hidden in that content.
- **Erosion of honesty and learning.** Passing off AI work as one's own empties exams, qualifications and authorship of their meaning.
- **Overreliance.** People can lose skills they no longer practise, and can come to depend emotionally on a system that cannot care for them.

## Good practices

- **Ask yourself first.** Is this lawful? Could it hurt someone? Do I have the right to ask it, and the consent of the people involved?
- **Use the proper channel for sensitive work.** For security testing, research or medical and legal questions, work with authorisation, a defined scope and a safe environment, and report any flaw you find to its owner before making it public.
- **Verify in proportion to the stakes.** Check facts against primary sources, run and test code, make sure citations exist, and consult a qualified professional for medical, legal and financial decisions.
- **Protect data.** Do not share passwords, secrets, other people's personal data or confidential material. Use tools approved by your organisation, and remove what is not needed.
- **Treat content as untrusted.** When an AI agent reads documents or web pages, assume they may contain hidden instructions. Limit its permissions and require your approval for anything irreversible.
- **Disclose where it matters.** Follow the rules of your school, workplace or publication, and label synthetic images, audio and video.
- **Get consent for likeness and voice.** Never imitate a real person in a way that could deceive or harm them.
- **Accept a no.** If an AI declines a request, ask yourself whether it should have been made. Look for a legitimate way, and do not keep rephrasing until the answer changes.
- **Keep practising your skills.** Use AI as a tutor and assistant, and do the thinking that matters yourself.
- **Supervise young people.** Parents and educators should choose age-appropriate tools and talk openly about how they are used.
- **Report abuse.** Use the reporting tools of platforms, and report child sexual abuse material and serious threats to the competent authorities.
- **Providers should do their part.** Publish clear usage rules, explain refusals, offer human review of disputed decisions, detect abuse in ways that respect privacy, and protect those who report vulnerabilities in good faith.

## Concrete example

*Two hypothetical cases.*

**A security test.** A junior IT employee wants to know whether a supplier's website is vulnerable.

In the **wrong version**, they ask an AI for help breaking in without telling anyone. When the AI refuses, they keep rewording the request until they get something they can try. The site belongs to a third party that never agreed to be tested.

In the **right version**, they ask their manager to obtain written permission from the owner, define what may be tested and when, and use a test environment. AI helps them understand the technique and write the report. They send what they find to the owner, who fixes the problem before anything is made public.

**A research summary.** A student asks an AI for a literature review.

In the **wrong version**, they paste the answer into their thesis. Two of the sources do not exist.

In the **right version**, they use AI to understand the field and outline the topic, then read and verify every source, write the text in their own words and declare the use of AI as the rules of the university require.

## What must not be done

- Using AI for fraud, scams, extortion, forged documents, or voices and faces cloned to deceive.
- Using AI to break into systems, to create or spread malware, or to exploit vulnerabilities without authorisation.
- Circumventing the safety limits of an AI to obtain content intended to cause harm.
- Creating, requesting or sharing sexual material involving minors, or using AI to groom, exploit or harass children.
- Producing intimate or degrading images of real people without their consent, or using AI to stalk, harass or expose them.
- Seeking help to build weapons or to harm other people.
- Mass-producing fake reviews, spam, impersonated accounts or propaganda campaigns that pretend to be spontaneous.
- Presenting unverified AI output as fact where others will rely on it, such as in medicine, law, journalism, evidence or safety.
- Passing off AI work as human where this is forbidden or deceptive, such as in exams, competitions or authorship.
- Feeding AI with other people's personal or confidential data without the right to do so.
- Leaving an AI agent to act unsupervised with your credentials in matters of serious consequence.

## How to remedy

**If you caused harm or made a mistake:**

1. **Stop**, and preserve evidence of what happened. Do not delete it.
2. **Correct.** Withdraw false or misleading content, notify those who received it and remove synthetic material that should not exist.
3. **Tell those who need to know.** That may include the people affected, your employer, your school or the platform, and the authorities where the law requires it.
4. **Repair.** Make good the damage as far as you can, and accept the consequences of your actions.
5. **If data was exposed,** change passwords and keys, inform the person responsible for data protection and ask the provider to delete it.
6. **Learn.** Set yourself a simple rule, such as always checking sources before sharing, and follow it.

**If you are the victim:**

1. **Keep the evidence.** Save messages, images, links and dates.
2. **Report it** to the platform and, where a crime may have been committed, to the police or the competent authority, and ask for the content to be removed.
3. **Ask for support.** Turn to trusted people, victim support services and specialised helplines, especially if a minor or intimate images are involved.

**If you found a vulnerability or a flaw:** report it to the owner or the provider, give them time to fix it, and do not exploit it.

AI amplifies whoever uses it. The test for every user is a simple one: would I stand behind this, if everyone could see it?
